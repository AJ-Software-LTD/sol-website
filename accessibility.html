Accessibility

The wonder of the night sky shouldn't depend on being able to see it, or on using a mouse. SOL is built on the Mac's own accessibility features, and every accessibility option in SOL is free.

Using SOL your way

VoiceOver

With VoiceOver on, SOL's menu becomes a Simplified Menu of standard Mac menu items. Each setting says whether it's on or off and what it does; each slider becomes a list of its options. The notable dates are read with their explanations.

Voice Control

Every control has a name to say. Sliders answer to "Increment" and "Decrement", by name ("Increment Speed") or by number. For choosing from lists, turn on Accessibility ▸ Simplified Menu.

Keyboard

Open the menu with Control-F8, then use the arrow keys. Space ticks a setting; Space or = and − or Delete move a slider; Return chooses; Escape closes SOL's windows.

Larger text

Accessibility ▸ Text size: Small, Normal, Large or Largest (double), across the menu and SOL's windows. Text is never smaller than 10 points, and every control is at least 20 points tall to click, at every size.

High Contrast

Stronger text, outlines and slider tracks. It's on automatically whenever your Mac's Increase Contrast is on.

Reduce Motion

Keeps the Sun still, stops flares, shooting stars and supernovae, and moves to a date at once instead of sweeping the planets round. It's on automatically whenever your Mac's Reduce Motion is on.

Never colour alone

Pro features are marked with the word PRO, a slider's Pro range is dotted, and with Differentiate Without Color the active badge reads ✓PRO.

The solar system in words

Explore the Sky ▸ Planets describes the wallpaper: the date shown and each planet's distance from the Sun, its angle around it and where it sits on screen. At a planetary alignment it says how tight the grouping is.

SOL follows your Mac's accessibility settings and never turns them off. Accessibility ▸ Mac Accessibility Settings… opens them, and the first-launch window offers text size, High Contrast and Reduce Motion before anything else.

The guidance SOL follows

  • Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Accessibility, including Apple's minimums of 10-point text and 20-point click targets on the Mac.
  • Apple's Accessibility Nutrition Labels criteria, which decide what SOL declares on the App Store.
  • WCAG 2.2 contrast ratios as a measuring stick: at least 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large text, outlines and icons.
  • Apple's accessibility frameworks for every control, with SOL's own controls named, labelled and given actions for assistive technologies.

Following this guidance is how SOL aims to do right by the people using it; it isn't a certification. SOL is tested by hand with VoiceOver and Voice Control, its controls are checked through the Mac's accessibility tree, and its text and control sizes are measured at every text size.

Known limitations

  • In SOL's standard menu, VoiceOver doesn't announce a change made with the keyboard until you move back to that row. The Simplified Menu, which turns on automatically with VoiceOver, avoids this.
  • With Voice Control, after switching between "Show names" and "Show numbers", close and reopen the menu to see the new labels. This is how macOS menus behave.
  • The moving wallpaper is a picture. Its meaning is given in words in Explore the Sky ▸ Planets and in each notable date's explanation.

Feedback

If something in SOL doesn't work for you, please open an issue on GitHub. Accessibility problems are treated as bugs and fixed first.
